Verdict
-------
The restored strong statement in Section 7.2 is now supported at the
paper's stated SnapPy/software trust level. The new calculation fills
the three dotted cusps before comparing covers. It therefore distinguishes
the five-component attaching-link exteriors in #3(S^1 x S^2), which was

optional strengthening of the diagram discussion, not a new premise of

Component correspondence and fillings
------------------------------------
I directly evaluated the PD-to-component mapping on all six supplied
PD inputs: old_fig1, new_fig1, old_fig2, new_fig2, and both older
archive-order spot-switched records. In every case the runtime component
order is C0,...,C7. The dotted cusps are consequently exactly 1, 2, and 4.
The source check of Link.exterior/KLPProjection confirms that these
component indices are transmitted to the exterior construction. The
record is CUSP_COMPONENT_MAPPING_FRESH.json.

For the four compact PD records, the three dotted-component sublinks
also simplify by Spherogram's basic Reidemeister simplification to the
unlink: sublink() drops one crossing-free unknot; the remaining two
components have eight crossings and simplify to two crossing-free
unknots. This is consistent with the dotted-unlink premise of the
Kirby diagrams. It was a small supporting check, not a new recorded
Reidemeister certificate.

The script fills the dotted cusps with (0,1), leaving each framed cusp
unfilled with (0,0). In the peripheral basis inherited from the link
exterior, this is zero surgery using the Seifert longitude. Both output
records have exactly five remaining cusps and H1 = Z^5. In particular,
this is not a comparison of the original eight-cusped S^3 link exteriors.

Why the stronger conclusion follows
-----------------------------------
Zero surgery on the dotted three-component unlink gives #3(S^1 x S^2).
The unfilled five components are the 2-handle attaching circles in that
boundary. A homeomorphism carrying one family of circles to the other
would restrict to a homeomorphism of their exteriors. Such a map would
give a bijection of all connected covers of any fixed degree, preserving
their first integral homology. The observed differing multisets obstruct
this without requiring a fixed ordering, orientation, or basis for the
circles. It therefore also excludes an identification by 1-handle slides
alone. It does not exclude a slide of one 2-handle over another; that
operation need not preserve the attaching-link exterior in the
1-handlebody boundary.

API/source check
----------------
The SnapPy 3.3.2 documentation for Manifold.covers describes enumeration
of all finite covers of the requested degree, with the low_index method
used by default for general covers. Manifold.homology returns the first
integral homology of the underlying Dehn-filled manifold. The argument
uses the combinatorial covers and integral homology, not a failure to
find an isometry or a difference of floating-point volumes.

Primary API reference:
  https://snappy.computop.org/manifold.html
The runtime and input identities are recorded in the fresh receipts.

    Standardness of the double branched cover of τ 0ρ1 P (−2, 3, 7)
                    A computer-assisted proof with explicit move certificates




                           Working draft – 28 October 2026 – Revision 4


                                                  Abstract
          Let J = P (−2, 3, 7) and let X0 be the double cover of S 4 branched over the zero-twist,
      one-roll spin of J. We give a computer-assisted proof that X0 is PL-homeomorphic, and hence
      diffeomorphic, to the standard four-sphere. The proof combines a geometric construction and
      reduction certificate identifying X0 with a six-pentachoron triangulation G6 , and a new explicit
      certificate connecting G6 to the double of a four-simplex. The latter consists of three paths with
      8,436 local moves in total: 7,842 Pachner moves and 594 admissible edge or triangle 2–0 moves.
      All 8,436 terminal moves have been independently replayed. A second implementation checks
      the admissibility and resulting facet pairings of all 594 2–0 steps without invoking Regina’s 2–0
      move routines. We state the geometric input, explain the cap replacement, and describe the
      verification dependencies. The resulting diffeomorphism answers Question 1.4 of Hughes, Kim
      and Miller in the affirmative. Combined with their periodicity theorem and results of Teragaito,
      it shows that Σ2 (S 4 , τ m ρ1 (J)) is diffeomorphic to S 4 whenever m ≡ 0, 2, 3 (mod 4).


1. The result and the certificate structure
For a classical knot J, write τ m ρn (J) for its twist-roll spin, with m the twist parameter and n the
roll parameter. For a smoothly embedded surface F in S 4 , write Σ2 (S 4 , F ) for the double branched
cover. We use the conventions of Hughes, Kim and Miller [1] and set

Miyazawa [2] used the two-knot τ 0 ρ1 (J) to construct infinitely many exotic projective-plane knots.
Hughes, Kim and Miller proved that Σ2 (S 4 , τ m ρn (K)) is unchanged up to diffeomorphism when m

is replaced by m + 4 [1, Theorem 1.1]. They deduced that the homotopy CP s arising as double
branched covers of Miyazawa’s projective-plane knots are standard [1, Corollary 1.2], and asked
whether X0 is diffeomorphic to S 4 [1, Question 1.4].
Teragaito’s unknotted examples τ 18 ρ1 (J) and τ 19 ρ1 (J) [3], together with that periodicity, already
settle the twists m ≡ 2, 3 (mod 4); see [1, proof of Corollary 1.2 and Remark 1.3(1)]. Kuhrman [4,

2. The geometric construction input
Let K0 denote the marked master triangulation specified in Supplement C1. It has 103,744 four-
simplices and 4,310 vertices. The following statement records the geometric content used from that
construction.

Construction input 2.1. The PL four-manifold underlying K0 admits the smooth manifold
X0 = Σ2 (S 4 , τ 0 ρ1 (J)) as a compatible smoothing. Equivalently, K0 is PL-homeomorphic to a
Whitehead triangulation of X0 .
Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3 reduce this identification to three marked geometric identifications: the source,
the relative monodromy, and the cap attachment. Section 2.3 explains how the maps and checks in
C1 establish them. Section 2.4 records complementary consistency checks.


2.1. A decomposition of the branched cover

Let (B 3 , a) be obtained from (S 3 , J) by removing a small open ball centred on a point of J, leaving
a knotted arc a. In the roll-spinning construction of Litherland [6], as described in [1, Section 2], the
roll monodromy R is a diffeomorphism of (B 3 , a) fixing a boundary collar. As a diffeomorphism of
the ball alone, it is isotopic to the identity relative to the boundary [10]. Thus its ambient mapping
torus is B 3 × S 1 , and the zero-twist pair has the decomposition

                          (S 4 , τ 0 ρ1 (J)) ∼
                                             = (B 3 , a) ×R S 1 ∪id (S 2 , ∂a) × D2 .
                                                                                  

The identity attachment is taken in the fixed boundary product coordinates. The roll is supported
in a collar T 2 × [0, 1] of the torus T 2 = ∂ν(∂B 3 ∪ a), where ν denotes a regular neighbourhood. In
longitude and meridian coordinates u, v ∈ R/Z, respectively, its collar model is

                                       (u, v, r) 7−→ (u + h(r), v, r),
where h increases from 0 to 1 and is constant near the endpoints. Since the longitudinal coordinate
is periodic, this map is the identity near both boundary components of the collar.

Lemma 2.2. Let V = Σ2 (S 3 , J), let V∗ be the complement of an invariant open ball centred on the
branch circle, and let R′ be the lift of R that is the identity on a boundary collar. In the marked
product coordinates on ∂V∗ × S 1 , there is a diffeomorphism

                                     X0 ∼
                                        = V∗ ×R′ S 1 ∪id (S 2 × D2 ),

and R′ is one longitudinal shear on the lifted collar.
Proof. Take double branched covers of the pieces. The cover of (B 3 , a) is V∗ , and its mapping torus
lifts to the mapping torus of R′ . The double cover of S 2 branched at the two points of ∂a is again S 2 ,
so the cap lifts to S 2 × D2 . The two lifts of the identity attachment differ by the deck transformation,
which extends over the cap. Choosing the identity lift in the marked coordinates gives the displayed

longitudinal shear remains one upstairs. For this knot, V is the Brieskorn homology sphere Σ(2, 3, 7),
up to orientation.


2.2. Relative PL models and compatible smoothings

Three-dimensional manifolds have compatible PL and smooth structures, with the usual uniqueness
and relative approximation results [8, 9]. In particular, the relative mapping class of a smooth
diffeomorphism fixing a boundary collar has a compatible PL representative. An isotopy fixed on
that collar induces an equivalence of mapping tori preserving the boundary product coordinates.
Gluing compatible models along the corresponding boundary maps then gives the PL structure
underlying the glued smooth manifold. The smooth conclusion for that fixed PL four-manifold uses
uniqueness of compatible smoothings [7, Theorem 3.5(2)]; see also [9, Part II, Theorem 4.2] and [10].

Lemma 2.3. Suppose P is a PL model of V∗ with a specified identification of its boundary collar.
Suppose g : P → P is a PL homeomorphism fixed on that collar whose relative PL mapping class,
under this marked identification, is the class of a compatible PL representative of R′ . If K0 is
PL-homeomorphic to (P ×g S 1 ) ∪id (S 2 × D2 ), with the standard cap and the identity attachment
in those product coordinates, then Construction input 2.1 holds.
Proof. Replace g, by an isotopy fixed on the boundary collar, with the compatible PL model of
R′ . The induced equivalence of mapping tori preserves the product coordinates on S 2 × S 1 and
therefore extends across the identity cap. The resulting PL manifold is a compatible triangulation
of the smooth union in Lemma 2.2. That union is X0 , proving the claim.

2.4. Consistency checks on the construction

The marked identifications above determine the constructed manifold. The following checks test
complementary features of the implementation.
The roll. With no roll, the same product-cap construction would give the spin of V , with fundamental
group π1 (V ) ̸= 1. By contrast, the full two-skeleton of K0 gives a presentation reduced to the trivial
group by recorded Tietze moves, including 60,487 generator eliminations. This rules out that no-roll
alternative. The relative monodromy certificate itself establishes the required longitudinal winding
one.
The cap. In the construction’s angle and time units, the unwrapped displacement of the m = 0
attachment is (0, 4), compared with (−8, 4) for the m = 2 control. The m = 0 attachment is the
identity in the marked boundary coordinates. The two caps differ in 3,008 of their 5,824 pentachora.
The exact attaching-map checks identify the filling class; the facet counts are consistency checks.
The source and control. The peripheral-preserving source identification fixes the knot pair (S 3 , J).
The m = 0 and m = 2 constructions share the source, exterior, roll and boundary adaptor. Their
masters have 100,736 common facets, and their vertex labels, deck map, and marked branch and
twin spheres agree. The separate reduction and terminal certificate below establish standardness of
X0 .

Remark 2.4. For m = 0, reversing the roll replaces R by R−1 . Reversal of the mapping-torus circle,
extended over the cap by complex conjugation on D2 , gives an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism
between the resulting covers. Reversing the orientation used to parameterize J reverses the
longitudinal direction and is covered by the same argument. Since the standard four-sphere admits
an orientation-reversing self-diffeomorphism, these choices do not change the zero-twist standardness
conclusion. Corollary 1.3 uses the fixed twist and roll conventions of [1].

3. Reduction to six pentachora

Proposition 3.1. The underlying unmarked PL manifold of K0 is PL-homeomorphic to G6 .


3.1. Simplicial contractions and bistellar moves

The first trace contracts 3,308 edges and reduces the master to a simplicial complex with 44,620
facets and 1,002 vertices. Every contraction satisfies the full link condition

                                       lkK (u) ∩ lkK (v) = lkK (uv).
For a triangulation of a compact PL manifold without boundary, this condition guarantees that the
edge contraction preserves PL type; see Nevo [11, Theorem 1.4]. The checks use the full face sets,
rather than only the vertex sets of the links.
A second simplicial trace consists of 629,874 checked moves: 957 full-link edge contractions, 50,016
bistellar 2–4 moves, 517,325 bistellar 3–3 moves, and 61,576 bistellar 4–2 moves. Its endpoint C has
1,332 facets, 45 vertices, and face vector

                                    f (C) = (45, 795, 2750, 3330, 1332).
The ordered facets at this endpoint agree with the generalized triangulation used in the next stage.
All 3,330 facet pairs, equivalently 6,660 half-facets, were checked across that conversion. Up to
this point the reduction is a closed-manifold PL equivalence in the simplicial category. It does not
transport the covering involution or the branch and twin markings.


3.2. Puncturing and the manifold with boundary

The reduction certificate verifies that simplex 25 of C, with vertex labels [3,19,419,504,918], is
embedded. Remove its open interior and write M = C \ int(σ). This leaves 1,331 pentachora, with
∂M = ∂σ. A native Regina trace of 5,173 macro blocks, containing 54,563 explicit guarded 2–4
kicks and recorded simplification calls, implies the existence of a PL homeomorphism h : M −→ T5 .
The first simplification produces 51 pentachora. The final endpoint is the five-pentachoron manifold
T5 named in Table 1.
This earlier native trace was replayed with its recorded block hashes in the specified Linux Regina
environment. Its simplification calls remain a software dependency of Proposition 3.1; they are not
expanded into individual moves by the new terminal certificate. The explicit boundary of T5 is
identified with the standard one-tetrahedron PL three-sphere of signature bkaagj. Nothing in the
argument at this stage assumes that T5 is a four-ball.

4. Local moves and certificate soundness
4.1. The permitted moves

The terminal certificate uses Pachner moves and admissible 2–0 moves about edges or triangles.
A Pachner move replaces one side of the boundary of a five-simplex by the complementary side.
The four-dimensional move types are 1–5, 2–4, 3–3, 4–2 and 5–1. Under the usual admissibility
conditions these preserve PL type [12].
An edge or triangle 2–0 move removes two pentachora meeting around a degree-two face and
identifies the outer facets in pairs. The selected face must be valid and interior, the two pentachora
must be distinct, and its link must be the appropriate doubled-simplex sphere. Further identification
conditions exclude closed chains of faces that would invalidate the replacement. For the closed
inputs here, these are tested as acyclicity conditions on the affected face-pair identifications.
Admissible edge and triangle 2–0 moves are PL-preserving local simplifications [13, Section 4.1 and
Appendix C]. Their guarded implementation and restrictions on face identifications are documented
in Regina [14]. The flattening quotient itself is not the homeomorphism: the local replacement
preserves the ambient PL manifold. The certificate uses these admissible operations directly.


4.2. Encoding and a soundness lemma

A record in the terminal certificate is a quadruple (a, m, i, b). The strings a and b are canonical classic
Regina isomorphism signatures. Decode a to obtain a canonically labelled triangulation; m specifies
the move type and the dimension of the selected face, and i is its index in that decoding. Perform
the guarded move and require that the resulting classic signature equals b exactly. Consecutive
records must have identical adjoining signatures.

The code Pk denotes a Pachner move about a face of dimension k: it replaces 5 − k pentachora
by k + 1, changing their number by 2k − 4. Thus P0 , P1 , P2 , P3 , P4 denote 5–1, 4–2, 3–3, 2–4, and
1–5 moves, respectively. The codes Z1 and Z2 denote the edge and triangle 2–0 operations, each

change, and output signature separately. The supplied certificate is pinned to the exact hash in
Appendix A.

Lemma 4.1 (certificate soundness). Suppose a finite sequence of records chains from signature
a0 to signature aN . Suppose each recorded local move is admissible and preserves PL type, and
its output has the recorded next signature. Then the triangulations decoded from a0 and aN are
PL-homeomorphic.
Proof. Each local move gives a PL equivalence from its source to its output. Equality of the output
and next-source signatures supplies a combinatorial isomorphism to the canonical representative
used by the next record. Composing these equivalences proves the claim. In particular, several
forward paths with a common endpoint may be combined by symmetry and transitivity of PL
homeomorphism; reverse-move logs are unnecessary for this inference.

5.1. Independent checks of the terminal certificate

An independently written full-path checker imports none of the discovery or supplied verification
code. It checks the endpoint signatures against separately specified constants, rejects malformed
records, tests the canonical source states, and checks the selected faces, guarded local operations,
exact count changes, and every next signature. All 8,435 distinct states are checked to be valid,
closed, connected, orientable, and of Euler characteristic two. The checker was executed with Python
optimization enabled; its essential checks use explicit failures rather than assertions.
A second checker treats all 594 2–0 steps independently of Regina’s 2–0 move implementation. It
checks the doubled-link gluing permutations and the acyclicity of the relevant face identifications. It
then rebuilds the surviving facet pairing by tracing through the removed pillow and composing the
explicit affine permutations. Every reconstructed target has the required signature. This checker
never invokes move20, has20, or twoZeroMove. It still shares Regina’s signature codec, skeleton and
validity routines, and simplex construction.
Finally, a dependency check reconstructs the capped endpoint recorded by the earlier reduction
from its actual facet-pairing data, checks all thirty reciprocal gluing equations, and confirms that it
is exactly the starting G6 of the terminal certificate. Thus the new proof attaches to the previously
verified reduction without a relabelling or endpoint ambiguity. This check also binds the relevant
completed receipts and source files by their recorded hashes.
The full independent replay took 28.4 seconds and the separate 2–0 reconstruction took 2.2 seconds
in the recorded Python 3.12.14 / Regina 7.4.1 environment. The hardened v1.1 supplied verifier was
also executed successfully with Python optimization enabled, in 26.3 seconds. These are observed
execution times, not hardware-independent bounds.


6. The smooth conclusion and the scope of the result
Proof of Theorem 1.1. By Construction input 2.1, X0 is a compatible smoothing of the PL manifold
underlying K0 . Proposition 3.1 identifies that manifold with G6 , and Theorem 1.2 identifies G6 with
the standard PL sphere. Therefore X0 =   ∼PL S 4 . A compact PL four-manifold admits a compatible
smoothing unique up to diffeomorphism [7, Theorem 3.5(2)]. This is uniqueness for a fixed PL
structure; see also the classical smoothing theory of Hirsch-Mazur [9, Part II, Theorem 4.2], with
Cerf’s theorem Γ4 = 0 [10] among its low-dimensional ingredients. Applying this to the compatible
smooth structure in the construction yields X0 ∼  =DIFF S 4 .
The conclusion is nonequivariant. It identifies the underlying smooth manifold, as asked in [1,
Question 1.4]. The reduction does not establish that the branch two-knot is unknotted, that the
covering involution is conjugate to a standard involution, or that the marked twin configuration is
standardized.

Corollary 6.1. For every r ≥ 1, the manifold Yr is diffeomorphic to the standard S 4 . The covering
involutions, transported to the oriented standard S 4 , form an infinite family of pairwise inequivalent
involutions under orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism. None preserves a Riemannian
metric of positive scalar curvature.
Proof. The connected-sum construction of branched covers lifts a summing ball meeting the branch
surface in a standard disk to a standard four-ball. Consequently, Theorem 1.1 gives

                               Yr = Σ2 (S 4 , Fr ) ∼
                                                   =DIFF #rj=1 X0 ∼
                                                                  =DIFF S 4 .
Miyazawa’s Real Bauer-Furuta degree calculation gives | deg(F )| = 3, and his connected-sum
formula gives | deg(Fr )| = 3r [2, Theorem 4.32 and Proposition 3.6]. These values are invariant
under equivariant diffeomorphism. They distinguish the covering actions and, by the positive-scalar-
curvature obstruction in [2, proof of Theorem 4.46], exclude an invariant metric of positive scalar
curvature for every r ≥ 1.
Here equivalence is taken in the oriented category: an equivalence is an orientation-preserving
diffeomorphism intertwining the involutions. This supplies standard total spaces for the family in
Miyazawa’s Theorem 1.7. Topological conjugacy of these involutions, or topological unknotting of
the branch two-knots, is not asserted. The exotic projective-plane embeddings and the associated

topologically equivalent involutions on the standard CP discussed in [2, Theorem 4.38] and [1,
Corollary 1.5] are a distinct conclusion.


7. Certificate extraction
The search combined growth by 2–4 moves, mixing by 3–3 moves and reduction by admissible edge
and triangle 2–0 moves, following the simplification strategy described by Burke [13, Section 4.1].
Searches from G6 and a standard reference reached a common isomorphism signature. Loop erasure
and extraction from the visited-state graph produced the finite paths in Tables 2–3. Discovery was
randomized; verification of the recorded certificate is deterministic.

The proof combines mathematical local-move facts with exact finite computations. The initial
construction identification retains its recorded source and peripheral transport semantics, geometric
collar and cap arguments, and branch-model checks. The original closed master has separate vertex-
link certificates. The closed simplicial contractions and flips have explicit traces. The punctured
stage retains Regina’s simplification implementation as recorded in C2. The capping step uses

For the new terminal segment, the full checker still uses Regina for guarded Pachner moves,

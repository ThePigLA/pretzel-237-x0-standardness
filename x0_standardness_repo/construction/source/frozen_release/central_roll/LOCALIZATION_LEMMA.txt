SOURCE-LINKED LOCALIZATION OF THE POSITIVE m=2 TWIST
=================================================

Status and scope
----------------
This is a geometric PL proof identifying the source torus-supported twist
with the finite puncture-cap attachment. It uses the relative PL regular-
neighborhood and boundary-extension theorems. It does not assert that a
global normal-disk-bundle trivialization of the saved N tetrahedra has been
enumerated or independently replayed as a finite cell map.

The source is the actual marked double branched cover constructed in this
project. The accepted trefoil controls are not premises for its geometry.
The independent finite inputs for this lemma are recorded in
central_roll/localization_inputs.json, with canonical hashes of the source,
cut, and corrected signed-diagram chart. The finite central roll is checked
separately in central_roll/independent_verification.json. The finite cap
family and its local F2/Phi pullback are checked under cap/.

1. Actual source pair and local normal orientation
------------------------------------------------
Let M be the closed oriented source, D its deck involution, and C the fixed
circle. In the compressed source, C is the full subcomplex on 18 fixed
vertices and 18 fixed edges. No tetrahedron contains two vertices in the
same deck orbit. Thus the fixed point set of the simplicial action is
exactly C: there are no additional fixed simplex interiors.

Every fixed edge has a circular normal link, and deck acts by a free cyclic
half-turn on that circle. The lengths are four for 17 edges and six for one
edge. Every fixed vertex has a triangulated 2-sphere link, with exactly the
two fixed directions of the core. Its quotient link is a closed connected
PL surface of Euler characteristic two. The fixed-direction links are the
already verified free normal circles. These checks give the standard
locally flat branched pair at every point of C, downstairs and upstairs.

N is the literal epsilon-neighborhood

  N = {x : sum of barycentric coordinates at fixed source vertices >= 1/2}.

Its equality with the saved clipped cells is tied to the exact parent
partition and determinant checks in peripheral/cut_orientation.json.
Because C is full, this is a PL regular neighborhood of the source core.
Deck preserves its defining function. This is a source-relative regular-
neighborhood assertion, stronger than recognition of an unmarked solid
torus by a native program. Its quotient is a regular neighborhood of the
locally flat downstairs core.

The actual removed puncture ball is the join of the fixed edge [204,228]
with the four-edge normal circle. Its boundary is the suspension of that
circle. The positive normal cycle is [56,150,58,148]. The four ordered join
tetrahedra [204,228,e_i,e_(i+1)] all have positive master orientation, and
the oriented core containing [204,228] evaluates +1 under the longitude
cocycle. Thus the puncture angle is the positive upstairs normal angle.
Deck adds one half-turn. In the chosen ambient orientation,

  orientation(M) = du wedge dr wedge dv,
  orientation(boundary N) = -du wedge dv,
  orientation(boundary E) = +du wedge dv.

These signs agree with the corrected diagram chart (u_old,-v_old), the
signed knot exterior identification, and the native handedness calibration.

1A. Enlarge the tube before prescribing the puncture chart
--------------------------------------------------------
There is a necessary geometric distinction here. The four actual puncture
equator vertices lie on the original cut torus, at cut level 1/2. Their
harmonic longitude coordinates are not constant. Thus it would be wrong
to require a single chart of the original N both to equal that boundary
torus chart and to turn the puncture into a constant-longitude normal ball.

Use the saved N-adaptor to enlarge N to N-plus, before removing the
puncture. Its outer boundary is the rectangular grid at r=1. The actual
puncture ball is now entirely interior to N-plus, and its marked sphere
is disjoint from the outer grid. The checked relative coarsening fixes
the puncture boundary and extends by identity over the puncture ball.
The filled compact N-plus has 1,580 tetrahedra, boundary equal to the 24
outer grid triangles, and every one of the eight puncture-sphere triangles
is an interior face. These incidence assertions are checked directly in
central_roll/mixed_profile_transfer.json. Attaching a product collar and
applying the certified relative ball replacements preserve its regular-
neighborhood pair type.

First push BOTH components of the original mixed torus shear into the
central collar. On the full source collar r=3*s_input in [0,3] put

  H0(r)=r/3,   H1(r)=clamp(r-1,0,1).

Use the same exact six-triangle PL scalar J(r,t) already checked for roll,
but set

  Psi_t(u,v,r) = (u+J(r,t),v+J(r,t),r).

The homogeneous forward and inverse matrices on all six cells are recorded
and checked in mixed_profile_transfer.json. They have determinant one,
commute with deck, and satisfy

  J(r,0)=0,   J(r,1)=H0(r)-H1(r),   J(0,t)=J(3,t)=0.

Therefore the central mixed monodromy (H1,H1) composed with Psi_1 equals
Psi_0 composed with the source mixed monodromy (H0,H0), in unwrapped
coordinates. Psi is identity on original N and on r=0, so it fixes the
actual puncture, including its contact circle with the original torus.
After this transfer both monodromy components are identity on N-plus,
and their support is the central collar 1<=r<=2. All uses of a framed
normal chart below are on this enlarged N-plus and its OUTER grid.

2. A framed pair trivialization exists with both boundary prescriptions
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The ordinary PL normal-neighborhood theorem, applied downstairs, identifies
the enlarged regular-neighborhood pair with an oriented normal D2 bundle over S1.
In dimension three this oriented disk bundle is trivial. One may prescribe
a standard local normal chart on a short core interval containing the
puncture; relative regular-neighborhood uniqueness makes this compatible
with the actual join ball. The interval chart preserves the ordered core
endpoints and the positive normal circle just checked.

There is also an exact PL torus chart on boundary N-plus: the outer grid,
with markings transported through the saved source adaptor. Its meridian bounds the normal disk and
its oriented longitude has degree +1 on the core. Start with a product
trivialization that agrees with the local puncture chart. Adjust its normal
framing to the marked longitude; any integer adjustment can be supported
away from the prescribed short interval. The difference between its
boundary map and the specified outer torus map then acts trivially on the two
oriented homology generators of the torus. Such a torus map is isotopic to
the identity. Extend that boundary isotopy in an outer collar of N-plus,
disjoint from the puncture. This produces a PL pair homeomorphism whose
boundary restriction is the actual torus chart and whose puncture chart
is the prescribed normal join chart.

Equivalently, the extension criterion is preservation of the meridian
class; the longitude fixes the framing ambiguity. The criterion follows
by first extending across a meridian disk and then across the ball obtained
by cutting the solid torus along that disk. Keeping a prescribed inner
normal ball is the relative version, obtained by the local chart and
outer-collar adjustment above.

Lift this downstairs pair homeomorphism through the marked double
branched cover. The boundary projection has the recorded form

  (u,v_up) -> (u,2 v_up),

so the cover kernel and meridian sign agree with the product branched
model. Choose a lift agreeing with the prescribed upstairs marking;
the other possible lift is a constant deck half-shift. Either choice
conjugates the meridional shear and the deck-equivariant disk loop in the
same way, so it does not change the transfer or its angular winding. A
phase can be fixed by the puncture join chart. The local branch-link models extend the
lift across the core. The resulting upstairs pair chart is equivariant
and sends deck to half-rotation of a normal disk. This construction is the
explicit theorem dependency; the software has not output this entire
pair chart as matrices on source cells. It is not required to straighten
the interior original cut torus; that incompatible extra requirement has
been removed by section 1A.

The primary PL normal-neighborhood reference is Rourke and Sanderson,
"Block bundles, I", Annals of Mathematics 87 (1968), 1-28, section 4,
Theorems 4.3-4.4 and the relative addendum 4.5. Their proof constructs the
normal blocks from dual cells of the locally flat triangulated pair and
gives relative uniqueness by ambient isotopy. The low-dimensional solid-
torus extension also has the usual disk-and-ball proof; see Hatcher,
"Notes on Basic 3-Manifold Topology", section 2.1, the explanation that a
meridian attachment determines the solid-torus filling.

  https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/rs003.pdf
  https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/3M/3M.pdf

3. Use a genuine PL disk loop, not a polar bilinear formula
---------------------------------------------------------
The finite cap family R_t on the sphere preserves time, poles, deck, and
the diamond radial coordinate on both hemispheres. Its equator is exactly

  theta -> theta - t,

and R_0=R_1=id. Radial preservation is an affine identity on every one of
the 1,792 recorded family tetrahedra. A hemisphere therefore supplies a
finite PL disk-isotopy loop h_t. This does not mean h_t is uniform angular
rotation at every radius or that {h_t} is a circle group action.

Put a scaled copy of h_t on an inner normal disk of radius a<1 in the pair
chart from section 2. Extend the boundary rotation through the outer
normal annulus using an equivariant PL product chart S1 x [a,1]. On that
annulus the map is (theta,r,t)->(theta-t,r,t). It is jointly PL in its
product charts. It matches h_t at the inner circle and the actual torus
angle at the outer circle. Both parts have time endpoints equal to the
identity and commute with deck.

Choose the standard local puncture as the suspension ball

  |u-u0| + rho <= a

in this inner normal chart (with the appropriate short interval rescaling).
The inner disk family preserves rho and u, so it preserves this ball
literally. Its boundary loop is the same radial disk loop on each
hemisphere. Relative regular-neighborhood uniqueness supplied the pair
chart identifying this standard ball with the actual source join ball.

If the finite cap's north and south hemisphere extensions differ from
this common disk extension, their difference is a disk-loop fixing the
equator, the pole and both time endpoints. That difference has a joint PL,
deck-equivariant Alexander contraction, as described next. Thus the
actual finite cap family represents the same marked boundary loop.

4. Parametric Alexander contraction with a joint PL formula
---------------------------------------------------------
Let delta(z)=(delta_t(x),t) on the convex 3-ball

  B = D2_diamond x [0,1],   z=(x,t),   c=(0,0,1/2).

Assume delta is PL, time preserving, identity on all of boundary B, fixes
the pole axis, and commutes with the half-turn. These properties hold for
the difference of the two disk-loop extensions after a common refinement.
For a in [0,1] define A_a to be identity outside c+a(B-c), and inside use

  A_a(z) = c + a [ delta(c+(z-c)/a) - c ].

At a=0 set A_0=id. This scales disk and time together; scaling only disk
radius in a time-dependent formula would create an unjustified bilinear
term. Here, if delta(w)=Lw+b on one affine cell, the displayed expression
is

  Lz + (I-L)c + a(Lc+b-c),

which is affine in (z,a). Its domain is the cone over that affine cell
with apex (c,0). Together with the complementary cells these form a finite
polyhedral subdivision. The inverse uses delta inverse in the same formula.
Boundary equality gives continuity. Since delta leaves t fixed, A_a also
leaves t fixed; it fixes the pole axis and time endpoints. The half-turn
commutes with the centered scaling, so equivariance is preserved.

This is a PL homotopy through disk loops, rel equator, pole and time
endpoints. It need not preserve every disk radius during the homotopy;
that is not required for comparing the boundary loops. Applying it on the
two sphere hemispheres gives an isotopy between the two S2 x S1 attaching
maps. The full equatorial winding is unchanged, since the homotopy fixes
the equator pointwise after taking the difference of the loops.

To make the matching of boundary loops a relative source map, use a small
added spherical collar with coordinate q from its puncture end to its
bulk end. Pull the contraction back by a=1-q and extend identity beyond
that collar. Because A preserves time, this is a joint PL map of collar
times time, with a joint PL inverse. It realizes the desired difference
delta at q=0, is identity at q=1 and at both time endpoints, and commutes
with deck. Its support is disjoint from the central roll. Thus the phrase
"after matching their boundary loops" below has a concrete PL collar
construction; it is not an appeal merely to homotopy of unmarked degrees.

5. Explicit central torus-to-deck isotopy and its source seam
-----------------------------------------------------------
After the source profile transfer of section 1A, use the marked central
torus coordinates (u,v,s), where s=r-1 increases from N-plus to E-plus.
The positive m=2 lifted twist f in this central representative is

  f(u,v,s) = (u,v+s,s)

on this torus collar and identity on N-plus and E-plus. The endpoint shifts s=0 and
s=1 are identity modulo the meridian period, while interior s is not.
Define K_t on the collar by

  K_t(u,v,s) = (u,v-min(1-s,t),s).

On N-plus use the PL normal-disk loop from section 3, and on E-plus use identity.
At s=0 the collar map is v->v-t, matching the outer normal circle. At s=1
it is identity, matching E-plus. The collar has two affine charts s+t<=1 and
s+t>=1; each has determinant one and an inverse obtained by changing the
sign of the correction. On N-plus joint PL and an inverse follow from the
finite disk-loop family and the fixed PL pair chart. Hence these pieces
give a joint PL isotopy of the punctured source, preserving deck.

Its endpoints satisfy K_0=id and

  K_1(u,v,s)=(u,v+s-1,s)=(u,v+s,s) mod Z,

so K_1=f on this central source representative. The integer -1 has been displayed rather
than discarded: it is exactly the negative full normal turn followed at
the puncture boundary. In the accepted unwrapped seam convention this
is the identity 1+(v+s-1)=v+s. The bulk deck square is identity as a map;
the path of the isotopy and cap winding still contain that full turn.

Let g be the positive roll, g(u,v,s)=(u+s,v,s) on the same central collar,
identity on N-plus and E-plus. The two maps commute, and every K_t commutes with g:
on the torus collar the corrections use separate u and v coordinates and
depend only on s,t, while g is identity on N-plus. With the convention

  (x,1) ~ (f g(x),0),

the map [x,t] -> [K_t(x),t] to the roll-only mapping torus is well defined,
because g K_1=K_0 f g. Compose first with the simultaneous (u,v) profile
transfer of section 1A to start from the original mixed source shear.
No commutation between that profile transfer and the global normal-disk
loop on N-plus is needed or asserted. The existing u-only central
certificate remains the correct roll-only transfer; the mixed version
is separately checked in mixed_profile_transfer.json.

The restriction of K_t to the puncture is the negative full normal sphere
loop. Section 4 identifies it, preserving its equator winding and source
puncture labels, with the actual finite beta2 family. Thus attaching the
finite cap to the shared even roll bulk gives the source m=2,n=1 closed
construction, under the explicitly stated PL normal-neighborhood theorem
dependency. This does not follow from deck parity or from homology.

6. Relation to the finite localized F2/Phi certificate
---------------------------------------------------
On the added spherical collar let q run from puncture to bulk. The cap
certificate uses the actual finite negative-turn family R_s and sets

  F2(x,q) = R_(1-q)(x),
  Phi(x,q,t) = R_min(1-q,t)(x).

These maps are PL by finite affine pullback cells and have explicitly
checked inverses. Their physical seam is Phi_1=F2, with identity bulk
deck square. At the equator the F2 lift is theta+q-1; the accepted positive
lift theta+q is obtained by adding the displayed integer one. Phi at q=0
is theta-t. The eight-angle/four-time cap path is therefore (-8,4), not
(0,4). No reduction of endpoint vertex labels changes this integer path.

The original torus-supported f and the spherical-collar F2 are relative
representatives of the same construction. After matching their boundary
loops, Phi_t inverse composed with K_t is an isotopy fixing the puncture
boundary. It starts at identity and ends at F2 inverse composed with f.
Both are deck equivariant and commute with the roll. This identifies their
relative mapping tori and the corresponding cap attachment. It does not
claim that the two pointwise source maps are equal.

7. Audit boundary
-----------------
Finite certificates establish the source, the signed marked torus chart,
the regular-neighborhood local hypotheses, the actual puncture normal
chart, the torus adaptors, central roll, finite cap, and local F2/Phi map.
The global extension from the saved boundary chart and local puncture
chart to an equivariant framed chart on all of the enlarged N-plus is a theorem-level PL
existence/uniqueness step. No finite matrix atlas for that chart is exported.
The source-linked lemma above is sufficient for the geometric identification
once this conventional PL theorem is included in the trust basis. A demand
for an entirely finite pointwise replay of the global source conjugacy
would require an additional normal-disk-bundle atlas; the present software
does not claim that stronger certification.

Nothing in this lemma proves PL standardness of X2 or authorizes the
guarded X0 construction. Those remain separate gates.

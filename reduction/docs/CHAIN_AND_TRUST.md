This module establishes the PL type of that precise complex. The geometric

## Elementary certificates

`source/elementary/audit_steps012.py` validates the schemas and source
identities assumed by three unchanged mathematical checkers. It verifies
every source facet has five distinct vertices, that there are no duplicate
facets, and that every exported facet pairing is exactly the identification
induced by common abstract vertex labels. The master and the contraction
endpoint use every half-facet exactly once and have connected orientable
dual adjacency.

The link checker reduces all 4,310 vertex links to the boundary of an abstract
four-simplex. Its 529,460 three-dimensional flips are individually checked.
Together with the complete gluing checks, this supplies the closed PL
four-manifold premise independently of Regina's link recognizer.

The prefix contains 3,308 edge contractions. At each step the checker requires
the full link condition `lk(u) intersect lk(v) = lk(uv)` and checks the exact
result. The endpoint has 44,620 facets. The subsequent log contains another
957 contractions and 628,917 bistellar flips, for 629,874 moves total. The
result has 1,332 facets and 45 vertices, with face vector
`(45, 795, 2750, 3330, 1332)`.

The supplied contraction checker's historical introductory comment mentions
44,728 facets. That comment belongs to an earlier control run. The current
inputs, actual replay, and outer acceptance gate all require **44,620**.
The imported code has been preserved byte-for-byte.

These stages rely on the standard PL-preservation results for admissible
bistellar moves and contractions satisfying the full link condition. They
use finite combinatorial checks implemented in ordinary Python. Python's
assertions must be enabled for the unchanged inner checkers.

## Exact handoff to the native stage

The elementary replay writes its actual ordered facets and induced native
gluing table. Both arrays, including their order, must equal the original
1,332-facet macro source. Every half-facet is checked again in the native
source constructor.

The macro certificate binds the original compressed source file:

## Puncture, native macros, and capping

The native checker verifies that simplex 25, with vertices
`[3, 19, 419, 504, 918]`, is embedded before removing its interior. The
punctured complex has 1,331 pentachora and the expected simplex-sphere
boundary.

The certificate supplies 5,173 macro blocks. It contains 54,563 explicit
2–4 moves; each receives a guarded legality check and must increase the
number of pentachora by two. Each subsequent `simplify()` is run with the
recorded random-engine reset, and its complete numbered facet-pairing hash
must match the certificate. These checks prove that the specified native
computation has been reproduced.

The internal operations of `simplify()` are **not individually listed**.
Their PL-preservation remains a dependency on the recorded Regina
implementation. In particular, the macro stage should not be presented as
an elementary Pachner-only certificate. The driver requires the recorded
Linux x86-64 CPython 3.12 engine binary before a full replay begins.

The resulting five-pentachoron complex has signature:

```text
fHMLQcabcdeeeecanbpaoazaobWbGb
```

Its boundary is explicitly isomorphic to the standard three-sphere with
signature `bkaagj`. The checker constructs a known ball as a cone over that
sphere and checks the attaching map, including permutation equations,
ordered affine boundary frames, and injectivity of the quotient embeddings.
It checks the complete face pushout and the numerical identity

```text
(1, 2, 10, 13, 5) + (2, 3, 4, 3, 1) - (1, 2, 2, 1, 0)
    = (2, 3, 12, 15, 6).
```

The closed result is directly isomorphic to the six-pentachoron interface
specified in the README. No simplification is performed after capping.

To justify recovering the original closed PL type, a PL homeomorphism of
the punctured complexes restricts to their boundary spheres. The resulting
boundary map between the original removed ball and the new cap extends over
the balls after subdivision by coning, the PL Alexander trick. This argument
does not assume that the five-pentachoron punctured endpoint is already a
ball and does not invoke a four-dimensional Schoenflies conclusion.

## Terminal connected paths

The terminal JSON has SHA-256:

```text
e9ba9f14831bb0da32043d7d93eb0148f0683e2a6e4f67d5156e8e9fc94adcdf
```

It provides these forward paths:

| Path | Moves | Maximum pentachora | Pachner moves | Admissible 2–0 moves |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `G6 -> W` | 6,814 | 22 | 6,322 | 492 |
| `S6 -> W` | 1,519 | 16 | 1,417 | 102 |
| `S2 -> S6` | 103 | 14 | 103 | 0 |
| Total | 8,436 | 22 | 7,842 | 594 |

`P_k` denotes a move about a face of dimension `k`. It replaces `5-k`
pentachora by `k+1`, changing the count by `2k-4`. `Z1` and `Z2` denote
the admissible edge and triangle 2–0 operations. There are no edge-collapse
operations in this terminal certificate.

The supplied v1.1 checker and an independently written replay checker both
execute all three paths. The independent replay checks canonical signature
continuity, move indices, local degrees and distinct incident pentachora,
separate legality and execution results, exact size changes, and exact target
signatures. All 8,435 distinct states receive validity, connectedness,
closedness, orientability, and Euler-characteristic checks. The PL-manifold
premise is propagated through the admissible moves; no claim is made that
every intermediate vertex link is freshly recognized as a sphere.

The separate two-zero checker tests all 594 `Z1`/`Z2` moves with its own
local combinatorial conditions and constructs their resulting facet pairings
without calling Regina's 2–0 move functions. It still depends on Regina's
signature decoding/encoding, skeleton queries, and simplex constructors.
Pachner execution in the full replay also depends on Regina's guarded move
implementation.

The small composition helper `terminal.py` derives the path counts and
intersections directly from the certificate, compares the fresh receipts,
reconstructs the fresh capped triangulation, and checks both its reciprocal
gluing equations and an explicit isomorphism to the terminal start. It builds
the standard two-simplex double directly. It is a binding check after move
replay, not a replacement for running the move checkers.

## Scope of the final claim

A full successful invocation establishes a connected chain of checked
PL-preserving transformations from the exact marked master to the standard
sphere, subject to the stated software dependencies. Combining it with the
geometric construction identification gives the intended standardness
statement. The usual smoothing correspondence in dimension four supplies
the smooth conclusion for the compatible smoothing; this program itself
checks the PL triangulations.

The proof is nonequivariant. No standardness of the branch knot, no
equivariant conjugacy of the involution, and no transport of the marked
surfaces through these reductions is claimed by this module.

## Provenance and reproducibility

Every imported file is mapped to its source archive and member in
`provenance/master_sources.json` or `provenance/terminal_sources.json`.
The original package manifests are retained as provenance, although only
the positive-proof dependencies are included here. The new driver does not
read historical success receipts to accept the current run.

The original-release inputs, selected candidate, imported checkers, certificates,
fresh outputs, and new composition code are separately bound by hashes.
The release manifest covers the shipped payload. These hashes establish
identity and detect changes; they do not replace the mathematical replay
or the mathematical interpretation of the construction.
